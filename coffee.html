Coffee & Tea Education Guide

Explore how bean origins, roast profiles, and brewing methods shape every cup served at My Cafe.

Arabica vs. Robusta Beans

Arabica (Coffea arabica): Grown at higher elevations (1,200–2,200 meters), Arabica beans develop higher natural sugars and complex acidity, yielding floral, fruity, and chocolatey notes ideal for Cappuccinos and Lattes.

Robusta (Coffea canephora): Cultivated at lower altitudes, Robusta offers higher caffeine content, fuller body, and rich crema stability—often blended in small proportions to give traditional Espresso shots extra punch.

Roast Levels Explained

  • Light Roast: Light brown, no surface oil. Preserves origin acidity, citrus, and floral notes. Best for pour-over filter brewing.
  • Medium Roast: Balanced sweetness, body, and mild acidity with caramel and toasted nut undertones.
  • Medium-Dark Roast: Rich chocolate and spice notes with subtle surface oils—our preferred profile for Espresso (Rs. 250) and Cappuccino (Rs. 350).
  • Dark Roast: Deep mahogany color with bold, smoky bittersweet flavors and minimal acidity.

Brewing Methods & Extraction Parameters

A practical comparison of common coffee and tea preparation techniques.

Step 1: Dose & Grind

Freshly Ground to Order

Coffee loses delicate volatile aromatics within minutes of grinding. We grind beans immediately before tamping to lock in freshness.

Step 2: Water Chemistry

Filtered & Balanced Water

Since coffee is 98% water, balanced mineral content ensures clean extraction without flat or overly harsh flavors.

Step 3: Microfoam Texture

Silky Steamed Milk

Milk is aerated gently below 65°C to preserve natural lactose sweetness, creating glossy microfoam for our Latte and Cappuccino.
